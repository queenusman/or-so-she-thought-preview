Chapter One

The Arrangement of Desire

Elizabeth has spent her life learning how to live correctly. The courtyard at dawn was quiet with the particular stillness of an estate that ran on unwritten precision. Her future had been drafted by others, yet every responsibility was so thoroughly understood that it had long ceased to feel like an imposition.

Until the morning she found herself standing before the registry portrait, tracing the gilded border where her mother's signatures ended and hers were soon to begin. It was not anger that moved through her, but something much quieter and far more disorienting: the sudden inability to tell whether the things she wanted had ever been entirely hers to begin with.

In Norwyn, decisions were not announced as commands. They arrived as assumptions so gentle that dissenting felt like forgetting your own language. She adjusted the cuff of her jacket and stepped into the corridor, aware of every footstep echoing against the limestone floor.
