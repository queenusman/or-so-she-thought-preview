EXPLORE THE KINGDOM OF NORWYN

A kingdom shaped by inheritance, tradition, wealth, and the people who have learned how to live within them.

"Norwyn is a kingdom with a long memory. Its traditions reach into ordinary life. Its institutions carry the weight of generations. Its wealth is visible in some places and carefully hidden in others."

For Elizabeth, Norwyn is not simply the country she lives in. It is the world that taught her what a life was supposed to look like.

Unit 04 • Society

04 — LIFE IN NORWYN

How People Live

Norwyn is not one experience. A royal household, an aristocratic estate, a city apartment, a village home, and a workshop can all exist within the same kingdom while producing very different understandings of what life is supposed to be.

THE ROYAL FAMILY

Public duty, private surveillance, arranged education, protocol, ceremonial appearances, and the permanent scrutiny of reputation.

THE ARISTOCRACY

Land holdings, inherited titles, country seats, elite academies, marital alliances, and subtle social influence.

THE MIDDLE CLASS

Civil servants, solicitors, merchants, academics, clinic doctors, and administrative business owners.

WORKING LIFE

Craftspeople, estate servants, agricultural tenants, factory machinists, and independent workshop owners.

RURAL NORWYN

Communities further from the capital, where weather, season, manual work, and oral tradition shape everyday life far more than royal gazettes.

Unit 07 • Period & Atmosphere

07 — NORWYN IN THE 1990s

The World Elizabeth Lives In

The 1990s is not simply a date printed on the cover. It is everywhere: in the technology, the speed of information, and the tactile texture of daily life.

TECHNOLOGY

Boxy computer terminals in government basements, bulky television sets, landline telephones with coiled cords, and analog radios.

COMMUNICATION

Handwritten letters on crested paper, crackling international phone calls, morning broadsheets, and mechanical fax machines.

TRANSPORTATION

Black official sedans, regional diesel trains, gravel country roads, and sparse bus lines servicing the high mountain passes.

NEWS

Evening broadcasts, print gossip columns, foreign press clippings, and the slow, deliberate leaking of royal engagements.

MONEY

Crisp banknotes, bank books stamped in ink, locked desk drawers, and wealth counted in estates rather than digital numbers.

DOMESTIC LIFE

Linen presses, silver polish, kitchen tea kettles on constant boil, and formal routine measured by grandfather clocks.

Two Kingdoms

Economic Foundation Inherited land, shipping routes, maritime trade Steel manufacturing, mineral extraction, modern banking

THE AGREEMENT

Why do these two realms matter to one another? Valenne provides the industrial capital and raw coal Norwyn requires, while Norwyn commands the deepwater ports and royal legitimacy Valenne craves. A marriage between Edward and Elizabeth is not a romance; it is a financial treaty that neither court can afford to dismantle.

Unit 12 • The North

12 — THE MOUNTAIN REGION

A Different Norwyn

The mountains are not a romantic paradise of freedom. They are another Norwyn, defined by their own rigorous social agreements, communal obligations, and ancient survivals.

GEOGRAPHY

Granite shelves, cold rushing rivers, pine ridges, and winter roads that can remain blocked by ice for weeks.

COMMUNITY & WORK

Work is shared because survival demands it: woodcutting, roof repairs, garden harvests, and mutual watching.

LOCAL AUTHORITY

The Queen's magistrates rarely ascend the pass. Authority rests with elders like Mama Elena, whose respect is earned through decades of memory.

THE RULES THAT EXIST HERE

Elizabeth does not leave a world of expectations and enter a world without them. She enters another set of demands entirely—demands that ask who you are when you have no title to shield you.

Unit 16 • Psychology

16 — NORWYN THROUGH ELIZABETH'S EYES

The World She Knows

WHAT SHE NOTICES

The tightening of her mother’s jaw before a word is spoken. The tiny tear in a servant's sleeve that shouldn't be there. The exact order in which dignitaries step into the room.

WHAT SHE DOES NOT NOTICE

That nobody in the palace ever asks her what she would prefer to study, wear, or say—because asking would imply that her preference mattered.

WHAT SHE ASSUMES IS NORMAL

That marriage is an arrangement negotiated between older people who understand finance, and that fear before a public event is simply the price of having bloodline.

WHAT BECOMES STRANGE

The sound of laughter in a mountain kitchen where nobody looks at the door when someone enters. The realization that permission was never a law—it was a habit.

NORWYN IS MORE THAN A SETTING

Elizabeth has spent most of her life inside one version of Norwyn.
Leaving does not mean leaving Norwyn behind.
She carries parts of it with her.

Dramatis Personae

MEET THE PEOPLE OF NORWYN

Elizabeth's life is shaped by the people around her, but none of them can be reduced to what they want from her.

They love her.
They need things from her.
They protect her.
They misunderstand her.
Sometimes they do several of these things at once.

ELIZABETH

Elizabeth has spent most of her life knowing what is expected of her.

She is a daughter, a royal, a student, and a future bride. She has learned how to read a room, anticipate a response, and make herself appropriate to the circumstances around her.

What she has not yet learned is how to recognize what she wants before everyone else has had the opportunity to tell her.

ANNE

Queen Anne is Elizabeth's mother and one of the principal architects of the life prepared for her.

She does not believe she is taking Elizabeth's choices away.

She believes she is preparing her for a world that will not forgive her for being unprepared.

EDWARD OF VALENNE

Edward enters Elizabeth's life as the man she is expected to marry.

He is intelligent, observant, politically aware, and more difficult to understand than the arrangement between them initially suggests.

ELIAS

Elias is one of the first people to notice the difference between what Elizabeth says and what she has learned to say.

He is not her answer.

He has a life, loyalties, ambitions, and reasons of his own for being there.

ISABEL

Isabel has spent years serving the royal household and understands its rules from a position Elizabeth has rarely had reason to consider.

She knows what the palace asks of people.

She also knows that people sometimes choose to give it more than it asks.

MAMA ELENA

Far from the world Elizabeth knows, Mama Elena introduces her to another way of living—and another set of expectations.

The Mountain does not turn out to be a place without rules.

It simply has rules Elizabeth has never learned.

ROWAN

Rowan's influence reaches Elizabeth through ideas before it reaches her through memory.

His work raises a question that becomes increasingly difficult for her to ignore:

What does a person carry with them when they leave?

The Making of the Novel

WHERE IT BEGAN

Or So She Thought did not arrive fully formed. It changed as I wrote it.

Characters became more complicated. Questions became less convenient. Scenes survived that I expected to cut, while others disappeared after revealing that they belonged to an earlier version of the story.
This is a record of some of what changed along the way.

WHERE IT BEGAN

Every novel has a version of itself that existed before the version the reader eventually sees.

Or So She Thought began with a different story, and Elizabeth was not initially the person I thought she would become.

Somewhere along the way, her story became more interesting than the story I had originally intended to tell.

BUILDING NORWYN

Norwyn had to feel like a place that existed before Elizabeth began questioning it.

Its traditions could not exist simply to obstruct her. Its people had to have reasons for believing what they believed.

A world becomes more convincing when the people inside it do not think of themselves as characters in someone else's conflict.

BUILDING ELIZABETH

Elizabeth's development was never meant to be a simple movement from obedience to rebellion. The more I understood her, the less satisfying that story became.

She could love the life she had been given and still question it. She could leave and still carry it with her. She could return without becoming the same person who had left.

THE CHARACTERS WHO CHANGED

Characters rarely remain exactly where an author first puts them.

Some become more sympathetic. Some become less. Some become harder to judge at all.

THE SCENES THAT SURVIVED

Some scenes survive because they move the plot forward.

Others survive because removing them changes the meaning of everything around them. These are some of the scenes that refused to disappear.

THE SCENES THAT DIDN'T

Not every scene belongs in the finished novel.

Some exist only to teach the writer something the reader never needs to see.

THE TITLE

Or So She Thought became the title because certainty is part of Elizabeth's story.

The phrase belongs to the space between what a person believes and what they later discover was incomplete.

Questions & Archive

BEYOND THE NOVEL

The ideas, unresolved questions, and living archive from the world of Or So She Thought.

THE QUESTIONS BEHIND THE STORY

HOW DO YOU KNOW WHAT YOU WANT WHEN YOU WERE TAUGHT WHAT TO WANT?

A person can be influenced by family, culture, class, circumstance, and expectation without their desires becoming false.
So where does influence end and choice begin?

CAN A LIFE CHOSEN FOR YOU BECOME GENUINELY YOURS?

We inherit more than possessions.
We inherit assumptions about what a good life looks like, what is sensible, what is respectable, and what is possible.
Sometimes we eventually choose those things ourselves. Does their history make the choice less real?

CAN SOMEONE LOVE YOU AND STILL DECIDE TOO MUCH OF YOUR LIFE?

Love does not always arrive separately from power.
Sometimes the person protecting you is also the person deciding what you need protection from.

IS LEAVING ALWAYS FREEDOM?

Leaving changes what surrounds a person.
It does not necessarily change what that person has learned to expect, fear, value, or repeat.

WHAT DO WE CARRY WITH US WHEN WE LEAVE?

A person can cross a border, leave a house, change their clothes, abandon a role—and still bring much of the old world with them.

CAN RETURNING BE A CHOICE?

Sometimes leaving is necessary.
Sometimes staying is.
Sometimes the harder question is whether returning can ever mean going back freely.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN NOBODY HAS TO TELL YOU NO?

The deepest rules are sometimes the ones we learn well enough to enforce ourselves.

WHAT DOES IT MEAN TO CHOOSE WHEN SO MUCH OF YOU WAS FORMED BEFORE YOU COULD CHOOSE?

Before a person can decide what they believe, they have already learned what to notice, what to expect, what to fear, and what to call normal.
Choice does not begin from nowhere.

ABOUT QUEEN USMAN

A novelist interested in how people become who they are.

Queen Usman writes fiction about the things that shape people before they fully understand they are being shaped—family, desire, expectation, relationships, power, circumstance, and repetition.

Her current novel, Or So She Thought, is a psychological novel set in 1990s Norwyn. It follows Elizabeth, a young woman whose future has been carefully prepared for her, and the increasingly difficult question of how much of that future she has actually chosen.

Her essays follow the questions her fiction leaves behind, looking at the ordinary ways people become who they are.
