The Premise

In the island kingdom of Norwyn, where royal traditions are woven into everyday life and the future of a kingdom rests upon the shoulders of its next queen, Princess Elisabeta has always known what is expected of her.

She has been raised for a life of duty.

At twenty-two, Elisabeta is the eldest daughter of Queen Ecaterina and heir to the throne. She has spent years learning the history of her kingdom, studying the art of governance and preparing for the responsibilities that will one day be hers. Even her future marriage has been arranged, with her engagement to Prince Alexandru of Valenne forming another part of the life everyone expects her to lead.

It is, by all appearances, a wonderful life. She has a palace to call home, a kingdom to inherit and a future carefully prepared for her.

And Elisabeta has never had much reason to imagine it could be otherwise.

But beyond the palace gates, Norwyn is a world of its own. Its streets are filled with people making their way through lives of love, ambition, disappointment and unexpected possibility. They have their own hopes to protect, promises to keep and decisions to make. And although the kingdom may look different from where they stand, its rules and traditions touch their lives just as surely.

As Elisabeta begins to see more of the world beyond the one she has always known, familiar answers begin to give way to unfamiliar questions. Things she once understood so easily become a little harder to explain. The life she has been preparing for begins to look different when she considers the lives of those around her.

Perhaps growing into the person everyone expects you to be is not quite the same as discovering who you are.

And perhaps the most important part of a life is not always the part someone else has planned for you.

DISCOVER NORWYN

Across the water, upon a great island with mountains, forests and winding rivers, lies the kingdom of Norwyn.

It is a land with an ancient royal dynasty, customs passed down through generations and a way of life that belongs entirely to its people. Here, the Queen holds absolute authority, the Crown passes from mother to daughter, and the kingdom's capital rests inland, at the heart of its political and administrative life.

But Norwyn is much more than its royal palace.

Beyond its grand halls and carefully tended gardens are bustling cities, familiar little towns, peaceful rural communities, busy workshops, farms, harbours and settlements connected by roads, rivers and trade. From the merchant opening his shop in the morning to the student making her way to class, everyone has somewhere to go, something to do and a life of their own to lead.

Some lives are filled with comfort and opportunity; others are shaped by harder circumstances. Yet together, they make up the kingdom and give it its character.

What follows is a little journey through the country in which Elisabeta's story unfolds.

The Island and Its Seasons

Norwyn is a large island, and there is much to discover across its varied landscape.

In some parts, mountains rise above great stretches of forest. Elsewhere, farmland spreads across the countryside, rivers wind towards inland settlements, and long coastlines look out towards distant shores. Each region has its own character, shaped by its surroundings and the people who call it home.

The land and its people have always been closely connected. Farmers tend the fields, forests provide timber, rivers carry goods towards inland centres, and coastal communities maintain the kingdom's connections with lands across the water.

Norwyn has three seasons: spring, autumn and winter. There is no summer. Instead, the year follows the gentle thaw of spring, the return of colder weather in autumn and the first frosts of winter, with periods of rain and quieter skies in between.

The seasons bring their own familiar rhythms. Winter means damp stone, cold rooms and the comforting glow of a well-kept fire. The easing of the rain brings changes of its own, as people return to outdoor work and the countryside takes on a different appearance.

And beyond the island, across the water, are other kingdoms. Among them is Valenne, whose royal family and commercial interests have long-standing connections with Norwyn.

The Crown

At the heart of Norwyn's history stands its royal dynasty.

Norwyn is an absolute monarchy, and the Queen is the kingdom's sovereign, holding supreme governing authority. The Crown passes through the female line, with the eldest daughter serving as heir to the throne.

Royal children inherit their mother's royal family name. When a Queen marries, her husband retains the title of Prince rather than becoming King, and the succession remains within the maternal line.

At the beginning of our story, Queen Ecaterina rules Norwyn. Her eldest daughter, Princess Elisabeta, is heir to the throne, while her younger daughter is Princess Ileana.

The monarchy is woven into the everyday life of the kingdom. It is not simply a matter of grand ceremonies and royal appearances. The Crown has responsibilities that reach into the administration of the country, its official records and the institutions entrusted with carrying out its affairs.

For generations, the royal household has been part of the story of Norwyn, shaping the kingdom in ways that extend far beyond the palace walls.

The People of Norwyn

Every kingdom has its people, and Norwyn is no different.

Its society formally recognises two classes: royals and non-royals. Royals belong to the ruling dynasty, while everyone outside it is considered non-royal.

But life among the non-royal population is far from uniform.

There are prosperous merchants with thriving businesses, university students preparing for their futures, skilled joiners whose work is known across their towns, and labourers who earn their living through difficult and often poorly paid work.

Wealth, occupation, education, family circumstances and access to opportunities all influence the lives people are able to lead.

A merchant and a labourer may share the same formal social status, but their homes, prospects and daily experiences may be worlds apart.

The distinction between royal and non-royal is an important part of Norwynian society, but there are many other things that shape a person's place in the kingdom.

The Capital and the Palace

At the heart of the island, away from the coast, lies the Capital.

It is the seat of government, the home of the royal family and the location of several of Norwyn's most important institutions. Its streets bring together official buildings, places of learning, commercial establishments and private residences.

And within the Capital stands the Royal Palace.

It is a grand residence, though one that favours elegance over unnecessary display. Its rooms are carefully furnished, its galleries maintained and its gardens tended throughout the year. Among its familiar spaces are the library, the Blue Drawing Room, the conservatory, the oak-panelled east gallery and the long hall in the west wing.

Inside, the atmosphere is one of polished wood, beeswax, soft wool and fires kept burning against the cold. The palace has its formal rooms and important occasions, but it also has quieter corners where ordinary household life continues.

For behind every beautifully prepared room is someone who has taken the time to prepare it.

Gardeners care for the grounds, dressers maintain the royal family's clothing, attendants organise arrangements, and household staff see to meals, fires and the many details that allow the residence to function.

The palace may be home to a Queen and her family, but it is also a workplace for the many people whose efforts keep it running.

Culture and Customs

Norwyn has a culture all its own, shaped over generations by French, Spanish, wider European and Cambodian influences.

These influences can be found in its architecture, clothing, food, craftsmanship, ceremonies and domestic life. Over time, they have come together to form something distinctly Norwynian.

There is a particular appreciation for elegance in the kingdom, though not necessarily for extravagance. Fine materials, careful workmanship and thoughtful details are valued more than unnecessary display. A beautiful garment need not be covered in ornament, and a grand room need not be filled with everything it can hold.

Formality has its place in public life, often expressed through precise manners, thoughtful presentation and established customs.

Royal occasions follow familiar conventions. Introductions are made in a particular way, seating arrangements are carefully considered, and official appearances follow established forms.

The Queen is addressed as Your Majesty, while princesses are addressed as Your Highness.

These traditions are part of what makes Norwyn recognisable, giving its public life a sense of continuity across generations.

Education and the Royal Academy

Learning has an important place in Norwyn, and the kingdom is home to several kinds of educational institutions serving students from different backgrounds.

Among its principal institutions is the Royal Academy of Governance, situated in the Capital.

The Academy provides education in governance and administration, preparing students for responsibilities within the kingdom's institutions. Its students include members of the royal family as well as those from outside the royal class.

One of the important subjects taught there is Structural Ideology, a body of thought concerned with order, organisation and the relationship between individual responsibilities and the needs of the whole.

Within this teaching is the doctrine of Alignment. It describes the ideal of a person whose understanding of order has become so well formed that they recognise what serves the whole without needing to be instructed at every step.

Students encounter these principles through lessons, discussions and practical examples. Their understanding is examined through formal assessments and academic panels.

For those who study at the Academy, learning is not simply about remembering established principles. It is also about understanding how those principles are applied in the world beyond the classroom.

Work and Trade

Much of Norwyn's life is built upon the work of its people.

Fabric and flour are among the kingdom's major trades. Agricultural communities contribute to flour production, while textile workshops produce cloth for households, businesses and the royal residence.

There is also timber production, joinery, carpentry, construction, gardening, tailoring, transport and administration.

The forests supply timber, while rivers provide routes for moving heavy goods towards inland centres. Coal and timber travel towards the Capital, where railway sidings and industrial works support their movement and use.

Across the kingdom, people work in family businesses, independent workshops, commercial establishments, institutions and private households. Some inherit their occupations, while others learn a trade or build a business of their own.

Norwyn's economy depends upon all these different skills and enterprises.

And beyond its shores, trade continues across the water. Commercial arrangements connect Norwyn's ports with businesses and workers in other kingdoms, bringing goods, opportunities and livelihoods together.

Food and Everyday Life

There are familiar comforts to be found in a Norwynian home.

Rice, beans and potatoes are common foods in ordinary households, forming part of the meals that sustain families throughout the year.

Prosperity can sometimes be seen in the smallest details. Buttered bread, for instance, is associated with greater comfort and wealth.

The royal household enjoys a more varied dining tradition. Soup, fish and tea may be served at ordinary meals, while formal receptions and state occasions call for more elaborate arrangements.

Daily life is shaped by the work people do, the responsibilities they carry at home, their education and the changing weather.

In larger residences, domestic staff undertake much of the work required to maintain the household. Rooms are prepared, meals are served, clothing is cared for and fires are kept burning.

A home that appears perfectly in order is often the result of many hands working long before anyone notices.

Clothing and Dress

Clothing in Norwyn tells its own little story.

What people wear depends on their occupation, prosperity, the weather and the occasion. A working day calls for practical clothing, while a formal gathering brings different expectations.

Royal and wealthy women wear carefully made gowns, often fashioned from silk and other fine fabrics. Plain cotton is worn beneath formal clothing, while wool provides warmth during the colder months.

Men may wear black wool for formal occasions, while women complete their dress with gloves and carefully chosen accessories.

Household employees wear uniforms suited to their particular duties.

Norwyn places considerable value on fine clothing, not only for its appearance but also for the quality of its fabric, construction and workmanship.

Its textile traditions are an important part of both its cultural identity and commercial life.

Technology and Transport

Norwyn may be a kingdom of old traditions, but it is by no means untouched by modern life.

Electric lights brighten its buildings, motor cars travel along its roads, and telephones connect households and offices. Newspapers bring reports from across the island and beyond, while press photographers capture important public occasions.

Cassette recorders and railways are also part of the kingdom's everyday world.

Yet older practices have not disappeared. Candles remain familiar, coal fires continue to warm homes, handwritten cards are exchanged, and paper records still occupy an important place in households and institutions.

Motor cars serve private and official journeys, while railways and rivers connect the Capital with other settlements and commercial centres.

In Norwyn, the old and the new exist side by side, each finding its place in the lives of its people.

The Royal Family and Public Life

The Crown is a familiar presence throughout Norwyn.

The royal family attends official visits, charity occasions, receptions, state dinners, public openings and formal ceremonies. These events are carefully arranged, from the order of arrival to the seating plan and the photographs later released to the press.

Such occasions allow the royal family to be seen across the kingdom and form part of its relationship with the public.

Royal marriages carry significance of their own. Beyond the personal lives of those involved, they can strengthen relationships between kingdoms, unite royal families and hold diplomatic importance.

For members of the royal household, public appearances and private responsibilities are closely connected. Their lives are lived within traditions that have been observed for generations, even as each new generation brings its own circumstances.

Valenne

Across the water from Norwyn lies another kingdom: Valenne.

It has its own royal court, educational institutions and commercial interests, and its relationship with Norwyn extends across royal, diplomatic and commercial life.

Its southern ports are particularly important to trade and employment. Agreements between the two kingdoms affect businesses, institutions and the many people whose livelihoods depend upon commerce across the water.

Among those connected to Valenne is Alexandru, Elisabeta's betrothed and heir to its throne.

His place in the Valennian royal family makes the relationship between the two kingdoms an important part of the world surrounding the story.

Though separated by water, Norwyn and Valenne are connected in ways that reach into the lives of their people.

Institutions and Records

Every kingdom has a history worth preserving, and Norwyn is no exception.

Across the island, institutions maintain official records and administer different aspects of national life. Some preserve the history of the kingdom, while others oversee the practical arrangements that keep its industries and public affairs running.

The Founders' Archive preserves historical records and is supported by the Founders' Trust, which also organises public occasions such as its annual gala.

The Inspectorate oversees the Timber Inspection Schedule. Under this arrangement, timber entering Norwyn's trade is inspected on four fixed days each year, according to the same standards and using official seals.

The Schedule is regarded as an example of orderly administration because the same requirements apply regardless of ownership.

It is also one of the examples studied at the Royal Academy when students learn about Structural Ideology.

Through its archives, institutions and official records, Norwyn preserves more than the decisions of its rulers. It also preserves the arrangements, work and ideas that have helped shape the kingdom over time.

BEYOND THE PALACE

And so, beyond the royal residence, the kingdom carries on.

Shops open in the morning. Trains travel between settlements. Farmers tend their fields, students attend their lessons, and families gather in their homes at the end of the day.

There are relationships to nurture, businesses to maintain, responsibilities to fulfil and familiar routines that give each day its shape.

Not every life revolves around the Crown. Not every story begins in a palace or ends within its walls. Norwyn belongs to all the people who call it home, each living within the traditions, circumstances and distinctions that shape their part of the kingdom.

For the characters of Or So She Thought, Norwyn is much more than the place where events happen. It is the world that has shaped their understanding of family, position, responsibility and the lives they might lead.

And while Elisabeta's story offers one way of discovering this kingdom, there is so much more beyond its pages. There are places yet to visit, histories yet to uncover and lives yet to meet.

The Characters

The kingdom of Norwyn is home to all sorts of people, each with a life, a history and a place in the world they call home. Some live within the royal palace, others make their way through the kingdom's institutions, businesses and bustling streets. Each has their own responsibilities, relationships and little corner of Norwyn to call their own.

Come and meet some of the people whose paths cross in Or So She Thought.

Princess Elisabeta

The Heiress

Age: Twenty-two

Position: Eldest daughter of Queen Ecaterina, heir to the throne of Norwyn and betrothed to Alexandru of Valenne.

Born into the royal family, Elisabeta has grown up within the walls of the Royal Palace, where her days have been shaped by lessons, expectations and preparations for the future awaiting her. Her education has included private instruction in Structural Ideology and formal studies at the Royal Academy of Governance, where she has learned the principles that guide the kingdom and its institutions.

She is courteous and composed, with a thoughtful nature and an eye for the smallest details. She takes care with her words, likes to explain her reasoning and rarely leaves a question without considering it from several sides. She also has a curious habit of counting things, often noticing details that others might pass by.

Elisabeta enjoys reading and keeps a private notebook of her thoughts. She is familiar with the ideas she has been taught and takes a genuine interest in understanding the world around her, even when it does not always make sense as neatly as her lessons suggest.

As the eldest daughter and heir, she has been raised to carry the future of Norwyn's royal family. She knows what is expected of her and has spent years learning how to meet those expectations. But there is still much to discover about the life waiting beyond the lessons, the palace walls and the future others have carefully imagined for her.

Queen Ecaterina

The Sovereign

Position: Reigning Queen of Norwyn and mother of Princesses Elisabeta and Ileana.

For many years, Ecaterina has worn the Crown of Norwyn, carrying its responsibilities with the quiet assurance of someone who knows exactly what her position requires. She is a thoughtful and measured woman who rarely rushes to fill a silence. When she speaks, she chooses her words carefully and prefers to explain her decisions rather than simply expect others to follow them.

Her appearance is always beautifully arranged. She is particularly fond of dark plum gowns with delicate silver detailing, usually accompanied by a pair of elegant gloves. She pays attention to the smallest details, from the way a dress is fastened to the posture of someone standing before her. Very little escapes her notice.

Between the affairs of the kingdom and the running of the Royal Palace, Ecaterina has become accustomed to a life in which there is always something requiring her attention. She understands the traditions of the Crown, the duties of her household and the many little arrangements that keep royal life moving smoothly.

But beyond the throne, she is also a mother to two daughters. To Elisabeta and Ileana, she is the woman who has watched them grow, guided their upbringing and shared their home. Yet the Crown is never far away. Even within the family, the Queen and the mother must often sit side by side.

Princess Ileana

The Second Daughter

Position: The Queen's second daughter and Elisabeta's younger sister.

Ileana is quiet, precise and dry in her humour. She is attentive to practical details and often notices things that others overlook.

She is familiar with the organisation of the royal household and regularly works with papers, records and household information.

Although she shares her sister's intelligence, she has a different temperament and occupies a different position within the family.

Safira

The Companion

Position: Elisabeta's cat.

Safira is a familiar presence in the palace. She sleeps beneath tables, settles wherever there is room and follows the movements of the people around her.

She has her own ideas about where she belongs, which do not always coincide with the household's arrangements.

Alexandru of Valenne

The Intended

Position: Heir to the throne of Valenne and Elisabeta's betrothed.

Alexandru is tall and fair, with hair that tends to come loose after a walk across a courtyard in the rain.

He is warm, socially confident and quick with a joke. He remembers small details about people, sometimes imperfectly, and is comfortable in formal company.

He is fond of horses, particularly a grey horse named Marrow, who has an unfortunate refusal to cross water.

Alexandru and Elisabeta first met when she was eighteen. Their engagement forms part of the relationship between the royal families of Norwyn and Valenne.

Ambassador Duvall

The Diplomat

Position: Valenne's ambassador to Norwyn.

A spare, silver-haired man of quiet manner, Duvall represents Valenne at the Norwyn court and attends formal occasions involving the two kingdoms.

Mr. Corrow

The Tutor

Position: Elisabeta's private tutor.

Mr. Corrow teaches Elisabeta Structural Ideology and conducts her weekly lessons in the palace library.

He is courteous, precise and fond of order. He values careful reasoning and takes particular satisfaction in a pupil who can defend an argument under questioning.

He has taught Elisabeta for four years and is familiar with her academic abilities.

Mrs. Hale

The Dresser

Position: Royal dresser.

Mrs. Hale is responsible for fitting and maintaining the royal family's gowns. She has worked in the household for many years and knows the practical demands of dressing its members.

Direct and observant, she is particular about measurements, fabrics and the condition of every garment. She often works with pins in her mouth and keeps careful records of the gowns entrusted to her.

The Steward

Administration

Position: Senior officer of the royal household.

The steward oversees domestic arrangements and communicates official instructions and information.

Courteous and restrained, he is accustomed to the routines and formalities of palace administration.

The Household Staff

The Workers

The palace employs pages, maids, attendants, dressers, stewards, secretaries, guards, cooks, gardeners and other workers.

Their responsibilities differ, but together they maintain the daily life of the royal residence. An attendant accompanies Elisabeta whenever she leaves the palace.

Professor Calloway

The Lecturer

Position: Lecturer at the Royal Academy of Governance.

Professor Calloway teaches the doctrine of Alignment and uses practical examples to explain the principles of Structural Ideology.

He is formal and composed. He carries a folder to the lectern but rarely needs to open it during a lesson.

Lucian Fletcher

The Joiner’s Son

Position: Student at the Royal Academy of Governance and son of Tobias Fletcher, a joiner.

Lucian is broad-shouldered and slightly stooped, with large hands and marked knuckles. He wears a grey jumper mended at both elbows, often keeps a pencil behind his ear and has a pale scar along his jaw.

He is observant, direct and unhurried. He answers plainly, uses dry humour sparingly and is comfortable admitting when he does not know something.

His family is connected to Norwyn's timber trade, and he has practical experience of wood and the work involved in preparing it.

At the Academy, Lucian brings a familiarity with physical work and materials into discussions often concerned with principles and administration.

Tobias Fletcher

The Joiner

Position: Joiner and father of Lucian Fletcher.

Tobias operates a timber yard beside the river. His workshop has undertaken work for the palace, including woodwork for the east wing.

He is broad and grey-haired, with large hands marked by years of manual work. He speaks slowly and has a dry sense of humour.

His work is part of the skilled craftsmanship that supports Norwyn's buildings and businesses.

A Note from the Author

Welcome to Or So She Thought.

Or So She Thought takes place in Norwyn, an imagined island kingdom with its own history, institutions, customs and way of life. Although its world draws on a range of historical and cultural influences, Norwyn is entirely fictional. It has been shaped for this story and the people who inhabit it.

At the centre of the novel is Elisabeta, a young woman raised within the royal family and prepared for a future that comes with responsibilities, traditions and expectations of its own. Through her experiences and relationships, the story explores how family, upbringing, duty and personal desire influence the way we understand ourselves and the lives we believe we are meant to lead.

I have always been drawn to the less visible parts of human experience: the thoughts behind familiar behaviour, the expectations we gradually learn to accept, the adjustments we make without always noticing, and the moments when something we have always believed begins to look different. I wanted to explore some of these questions through fiction, and they became part of the foundation of this story.

For readers who enjoy discovering the details of an imagined world, I have included The World of Norwyn, a character guide and The Norwyn Archives. These offer additional glimpses into the kingdom, its people and its institutions. They are entirely optional, however. You can follow the story through the chapters alone and return to the additional material whenever you wish.

I hope you enjoy your time in Norwyn, find something to appreciate in its people, and perhaps recognise a few things about the world beyond it.

Thank you for giving this story your time.

Queen Usman
