The Premise

An introduction to the story.

Born into the royal family of Norwyn, Princess Elisabeta has never had to imagine a life outside the one prepared for her. Her world has its own order, traditions and expectations, along with a clear understanding of what makes a good life. Within it, almost everything has an explanation, and almost every decision has its proper place.

She has grown up knowing what is expected of her, what her future is supposed to look like and what it means to belong to the family that will one day shape the kingdom's future.

But as marriage, family relationships and the responsibilities of the Crown bring these expectations into sharper focus, Elisabeta begins to confront questions she has never had much reason to ask.

What happens when the life you have always understood begins to look different from the inside? When does duty become something you believe in, and when is it simply something you have learned to accept? How do you distinguish between what you genuinely want and what you have spent your life learning to want?

Her difficulty is not simply deciding whether to follow the life expected of her or turn away from it. It is understanding how much of her sense of herself, her family and the world has been shaped by the very life she is beginning to question.

Or So She Thought is a literary psychological novel about family, identity, duty and the complicated process of understanding a life you have always known.

THE WORLD OF NORWYN

Discover the kingdom behind the story.

Every story has a world beyond its characters. A place with its own history, traditions and ways of life. A place where people grow up learning what is familiar, what is expected of them and where they belong.

Norwyn is an island kingdom ruled by an absolute monarchy. The royal family holds a prominent place in its society, but the kingdom extends far beyond the palace. Its towns, households and places of work are home to people whose lives are shaped by their own circumstances, relationships and responsibilities.

Tradition, family and social position influence much of life in Norwyn. They affect how people understand their place in society, what opportunities are available to them and what others expect them to become.

This is the kingdom in which Or So She Thought takes place.

1. THE KINGDOM

Norwyn is an island kingdom with its own monarchy and established traditions. Its capital, known simply as the Capital, is situated inland.

Beyond the royal residence are towns and settlements where people live, work and trade. These communities form part of a wider society connected by the movement of people and goods.

Although the palace is central to the kingdom's public life, it is only one part of Norwyn. The country has a life of its own beyond the royal household, shaped by the everyday activities of the people who live there.

02. THE CROWN AND THE MONARCHY

Norwyn is governed by an absolute monarchy, with the Crown holding the highest authority in the kingdom.

The throne passes through the female line. Succession follows the maternal royal lineage, making the queen's family line central to the continuation of the monarchy.

This principle is reflected in the royal family's naming tradition. Children born into the family inherit their mother's royal family name, preserving the maternal line across generations.

There is also a distinction in the titles held within the royal marriage. The husband of a reigning queen remains a Prince rather than becoming King.

These traditions shape the structure of the monarchy and influence how the royal family understands inheritance, position and continuity.

03. THE ROYAL FAMILY AND SOCIETY

In Norwyn, society is organised around two principal social classes: royals and non-royals.

Being born into the royal family means belonging to an institution with a position of its own. Royal birth brings privileges, but it also carries responsibilities and expectations that extend beyond the individual.

The wider population belongs to the non-royal class. Within it, however, people live under different circumstances. Family background, occupation, personal relationships and access to resources all contribute to the differences between households.

Social position influences life in Norwyn, but it does not tell the whole story of an individual's circumstances.

04. THE LAND AND ITS PLACES

The Capital lies inland, while towns and settlements make up the wider landscape of Norwyn.

People move between these places for work, trade and other everyday reasons. Goods travel between communities, connecting different parts of the kingdom through their economic and social activities.

The locations encountered in Or So She Thought offer different views of Norwyn. Some are closely associated with the royal household, while others belong to the wider life of the country.

Each provides a different perspective on the kingdom and the people who inhabit it.

05. THE SEASONS

Norwyn has three seasons:

  • Spring
  • Autumn
  • Winter

There is no summer.

These seasons mark the passage of the year and form the natural calendar of the kingdom.

06. FOOD AND EVERYDAY LIFE

The everyday life of a place can often be understood through ordinary details. In Norwyn, food offers a glimpse into the differences between households.

Rice, beans and potatoes are common foods. Buttered bread, however, is associated with greater wealth. What might appear to be a small difference in daily meals can reflect the circumstances in which people live.

Not every distinction in Norwyn is visible through titles or formal displays of status. Some are found in household routines, the things people can afford and the comforts available to them.

These ordinary details help establish the material world in which the characters live.

07. WORK AND TRADE

Work and trade are part of what keeps Norwyn's communities connected. Fabric and flour are among the kingdom's major trades, linking the production and exchange of goods across different parts of the country.

People work in different occupations to support their households and contribute to the wider economy. Their activities form part of the everyday life that continues beyond the affairs of the royal family.

The kingdom is sustained not only by its institutions, but also by the work of the people who live within it.

08. ARCHITECTURE AND APPEARANCE

Norwyn has a visual identity influenced by several architectural and aesthetic traditions, including French, Spanish and Cambodian influences.

These influences contribute to the appearance of its buildings and surroundings, while the kingdom retains a character of its own.

Norwyn is a fictional kingdom, not a representation of any particular real country or historical period. Its appearance and cultural identity belong to the world of the novel.
