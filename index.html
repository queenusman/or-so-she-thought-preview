The Founding Era • Chapter One

Before Norwyn

The mud had ruined Victoria's hem by noon, but she refused to let the boy brush it away.

"Leave it," she said, leaning over the raw pine table. "If Lord Valenne thinks I have dry shoes, he'll assume the crossing was easy. If he thinks the crossing was easy, he will demand the toll bridges on the east bank."

There was no throne room. There was merely an abandoned storehouse whose roof had survived the winter fire. The four families sat upon salt barrels, each keeping one hand within reach of their daggers while their clerks argued over water rights. Everyone in the province was tired of burying cousins.

"We make the marriage treaty for twenty years," Valenne’s envoy muttered. "Not thirty. Twenty."

"Make it hereditary," Victoria replied without lifting her head from the surveyor's grid. Her eyes were bloodshot from four nights without sleep.

"Hereditary?" the clerk gasped. "Your Grace, that binds generations who haven't yet been born."

"Generations who haven't been born don't have to carry stretchers," Victoria said. "Bind it so hard that by the time our grandchildren are old enough to dispute it, they will think God wrote it into the limestone."

That was how Norwyn began: not with holy visions or royal bloodlines, but with a tired woman signing away three centuries of freedom to buy one year of quiet bread.

The Crowned Era • Chapter One

The Weight of Consent

Nobody had ever raised a voice against Elizabeth.

That was the detail historians in the capital would fail to understand when they eventually gathered her private ledgers. There had been no locked chamber, no harsh words at breakfast, no severe grandfather demanding total submission under pain of exile. Tyranny, if that was what one wished to call the arrangement of her existence, had entered through warmth.

Her mother, Queen Anne, arrived each morning at eight with a cup of steeped chamomile and two minutes of silence. Anne did not command. She asked questions that had already been resolved seventy years before either of them took their first breath.

"Are the eastern robes comfortable for the Valenne delegation, Elizabeth, or would you prefer the grey silk?"

It was always a choice between two variations of the same outcome. If Elizabeth chose the grey silk, it proved she possessed modesty. If she chose the eastern robes, it proved she possessed diplomatic instincts. There was no third option where she simply walked through the west terrace in yesterday's wool coat and let the horses eat uninspected grass.

"Edward arrived before noon," Anne said softly, resting one hand upon the back of Elizabeth's dressing chair. The touch was genuine. Anne did not wear rings on her left hand because she believed cold metal had no place against a daughter's skin. "He brought the trade charters from the river valley. He insisted on leaving them in the lower library so you wouldn't feel hurried."

"He is always thoughtful," Elizabeth said.

The sentence formed without friction. She had said it at twelve; she had said it at sixteen; she said it now at nineteen. It was entirely true. Edward of Valenne was thoughtful. He had memorized the names of her childhood dogs; he remembered that she disliked spiced wine; he never interrupted her when she spoke on treaty clauses.

That was what made her heart seize in the middle of the chest. It would have been so clean to hate him. If Edward had been insolent, if he had reeked of vanity or cruelty, refusal would have had the clear, sharp dignity of courage. Instead, he was good. He was plausible. He was everything someone who loved her would have chosen for her.

And because she was kind, and because she was sensible, she had slowly learned to want what everyone had decided she was meant to have.

Until the letter from the mountain border arrived with no royal seal at all.

Record #0012-A • Treaty of the First Stones

Edict on the Consanguine League of Valenne

"Let it be inscribed: Henceforth, Norwyn and the House of Valenne shall govern under marital concord. The royal heir shall betroth the direct issue of Valenne at their eighteenth summer, assuring unbroken sovereignty and the defense of the northern trade gates."

Private Diary Fragment: Queen Victoria (Founding Era)

"We wrote the eighteenth summer because Lord Caspar was ninety years old and his heir had a lung rot; we had to buy five years of quiet before Valenne's cousins could organize a siege. I never intended this calendar to govern my great-grandchildren. A crutch is meant to be discarded when the bone knits. Instead, they have gold-leafed the crutch."

Record #0087-C • Royal Nursery Directive

Memorandum on Elizabeth’s Schedule

"The Princess should be instructed in continental cadence, state economics, and music. Under no circumstance should her schedule permit unregulated retreat. Solitude invites melancholy in future rulers; purpose is the greatest armor a daughter of Norwyn can wear."

Anne's Note to the Governess (Found in private desk)

"Do not let her think this is punishment. I spent my eighteenth year trapped in a carriage while councilors debated whether I was sane enough to inherit. If Elizabeth knows the answer before they can frame the question, nobody will ever dare question her mind. Keep her busy so she does not bleed."
